# UAE + GCC Day-Surgery / Short-Stay Surgical Center Deal Sheet
**Lane:** Buy + operate, OR operator partnership (revenue share / management contract)
**Compiled:** 29 July 2026
**Deal sizes:** AED 1.5M – 17M ($400K – $4.6M) for facilities; operator partnership deals often zero-equity for operator
**Multiples (UAE):** 4–7x EBITDA SME · 6–14x for chains · 8–12x Western comparables
**Asset class:** DHA/DOH/MOHAP-licensed facilities, often with active insurance panels, distressed or founder-exit sales

---

## 1. The asset class in 60 seconds

**Day-surgery / short-stay surgical centers** = licensed facilities where patients are admitted, operated on, and discharged the same day. Specialty mix: ortho, ENT, dental, ophthalmology, GI endoscopy, plastic/aesthetic, vascular, general surgery, pain management, gynecology (D&C, lap), urology (minor).

**Why this lane is hot right now:**
- **Demand is structural** — 60%+ of all surgeries in Dubai are day-cases; payers (incl. Saudi DRG) are pushing volume out of inpatient
- **DHA / DOH / MOHAP regulators actively licensing new facilities** — the regulatory ceiling is high but reachable
- **Fragmented supply** — hundreds of single-site owner-operators, mostly distress sales (relocation, partnership breakup, visa issues, financial stress)
- **Asset-light economics** — 6,000 sq ft can generate AED 15L/month revenue; break-even in 6–18 months
- **The "operator problem"** — owners can secure a license and fit out, but cannot run clinical operations + insurance panels + surgeon recruitment. **This is exactly the gap your operating partner fills.**

**Two distinct play types:**

| Play | Capital required | What you do | Time to first revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buy + operate** | AED 1.5M – 17M | Acquire license, equipment, fit-out, lease. Install operator. Run it. | 2–4 months (if license transfer fast-track) |
| **Operator partnership (no equity)** | Zero | Take operational responsibility + revenue share. Owner keeps license + asset. | 30–60 days |

## 3. Deal economics — what to model

### Acquisition math (UAE day surgery)

**Setup cost (greenfield):**
- Fit-out: AED 500–800/sq ft × 4,000–8,000 sq ft = **AED 2M – 6.4M**
- Medical equipment (2 OR, 4 OPD, lab, recovery, CSSD, imaging): **AED 3M – 6M**
- DHA licensing + DataFlow + 12 months working capital: **AED 1M – 2M**
- **Total greenfield: AED 6M – 14.4M ($1.6M – $3.9M)**

**Distress sale price (existing licensed facility):**
- Small aesthetic clinic: AED 350K – 600K (60-75% off greenfield)
- Laser/derm clinic: AED 700K – 1.5M
- **Polyclinic / Day Surgery: AED 1.5M – 4M (50-70% off greenfield)**
- Large day surgery with revenue: AED 5M – 17M

**Operating economics (year 1-2):**
- Revenue at maturity: AED 3M – 12M/yr (depending on specialty mix)
- EBITDA margin at maturity: 15–25% (well-run)
- Break-even: 6–18 months post-close
- Typical UAE healthcare multiple paid: 4–7x EBITDA (SME), 6–14x (chain)

**Operator partnership deal (no equity):**
- Owner typically offers: management fee (3-6% of revenue) + perf bonus (5-10% of EBITDA above hurdle) + ESOP/equity kicker (5-15%)
- Operator brings: clinical leadership, surgeon network, insurance panel access, ops playbook, marketing
- Operator compensation: typically AED 30K – 80K/month base + 10-20% of EBITDA over hurdle

### India operator partnership (Pristyn Care model)

- Pristyn Care-style partner hospital structure: hospital provides OR + beds, Pristyn brings patients + brand + insurance
- Operator runs the partner hospital for 30-50% of net profit on Pristyn-routed cases
- Asset-light: no capex, no license transfer, no lease risk
- Revenue share: 60-70% to hospital, 30-40% to Pristyn/operator on Pristyn cases

### The "off-market" channel (where the best deals are)

Most good UAE day-surgery deals never hit public portals. They go through:
1. **DHA / DOH facility license transfer applications** — regulators see all ownership change requests, often refer to operator candidates
2. **Equipment vendors and lessors** — Getinge, Maquet, Mindray, Siemens Healthineers reps know which facilities are struggling
3. **Insurance panel managers** — DAMAN, ADNIC, Daman, Thiqa, AXA see claims patterns and owner distress signals
4. **Medical malpractice insurers** — know which doctors are struggling
5. **Real estate brokers specializing in healthcare leases** — Better Homes, Allsop, Chestertons healthcare division
6. **Law firm healthcare practices** — Al Tamimi, Clyde & Co, Hadef & Partners
7. **Hospital management companies** — NMC (post-restructuring), Aster, Mediclinic have operator candidate lists
8. **Indian business community networks in UAE** — ICAI Dubai, BCCI Dubai, IMA UAE chapters

## 6. Due diligence checklist (before you sign anything)

**License & regulatory:**
- [ ] Verify DHA / DOH / MOHAP facility license active, no violations, no patient complaints
- [ ] Check insurance panel continuity (DAMAN, AXA, ADNIC, Daman, Thiqa) — can they assign to new owner?
- [ ] For asset deal: confirm new license is needed in buyer's entity name (not just name change)
- [ ] For share deal: confirm UBO change notification is sufficient
- [ ] All clinical staff DHA-licensed + linked to facility + 3+ months in current emirate (for transfers)
- [ ] NABIDH / Malaffi integration current (Dubai/Abu Dhabi EHR platforms)
- [ ] ADHICS compliance (Abu Dhabi)
- [ ] No pending DHA fines or violations
- [ ] Medical director succession plan documented

**Commercial:**
- [ ] 3 years audited P&L (or tax returns + bank statements for 24 months)
- [ ] Revenue mix: insurance % vs cash pay, by payer
- [ ] Doctor/consultant exclusivity arrangements
- [ ] Patient database portability (UAE Data Protection Law compliant)
- [ ] Insurance AR aging report
- [ ] Lease: remaining term, renewal option, rent review clause, fit-out clause, landlord NOC for transfer
- [ ] All equipment fully paid (no finance/lease outstanding)
- [ ] No outstanding supplier payments, no staff end-of-service benefits liability

**Clinical:**
- [ ] Clinical governance: medical director, quality committee, infection control
- [ ] Accreditation status (JCI, NABH, ACI — or local DHA standards)
- [ ] Incident reports, near-miss logs
- [ ] Surgeon credentials + privilege list
- [ ] Surgical case mix, ASA distribution, complication rate

**Legal:**
- [ ] NOC from existing partners/shareholders
- [ ] No outstanding litigation, no plaintiff cases
- [ ] IP / brand / trademark ownership clear
- [ ] Patient consent forms and data handling compliant with UAE Data Law
- [ ] Non-compete clauses on existing doctors
- [ ] Confidentiality / non-solicit on existing staff

**Financial valuation sanity-check:**
- [ ] Asking price vs 4-7x EBITDA benchmark
- [ ] Working capital normalized
- [ ] Owner discretionary expenses added back
- [ ] Deferred maintenance capex identified
- [ ] Lease transfer cost factored
- [ ] License transfer + DataFlow cost budgeted (AED 4,000–18,000)

## 9. Red flags to walk away from

- Asking price > 7x EBITDA (unless strategic premium is justified)
- Owner can't produce 24 months bank statements
- Active DHA violations or pending patient complaint
- Single-practitioner dependency (one surgeon does 60%+ of revenue)
- Lease < 3 years remaining without renewal option
- Insurance panel in dispute with major payer
- Equipment > 7 years old and not maintained
- "Walk-in ready" but no patients in last 90 days
- Owner refuses to provide tax returns
- License category doesn't match services being delivered
- Any pending malpractice case
